  - **Path, not endpoint** — hence levels.
- **The matrix (the graphic — put it on screen):** Performance 0–5 ×
  Narrow/General. Emerging = any unskilled human. Competent = **50th
  percentile of skilled adults**. Expert = 90th. Exceptional (née

  of familiar names all the way up (AlphaFold sits at Superhuman-Narrow);
  **General column is empty above Level 1.**

  General — "Emerging AGI"** — Competent on SOME tasks (short essays,

### A2 — The other half of the curve: who AI hurts first (All, ≤8 min)

  Human Behaviour* (2025): GPT-4 with your demographics gets **+81%

  participants): the tuning that makes models MORE persuasive makes them
  **less factually accurate**. **Honest note the show should say out

  intelligence — the machine beats everyone.** The asymmetry starts at
  detection.
- **Exhibit 2 — detection is where the gap opens:** iProov (⚠ vendor
  study): **0.1%** of 2,000 adults correctly identified every deepfake;
  **39% of 65+ had never heard of deepfakes**. And the fraud data shows
  where that lands: FBI IC3 2024 — 60+ victims lost **$4.9B** (+43%
  YoY); FTC (Dec 2025) — reported 60+ losses **quadrupled since 2020**,
  true losses estimated up to **$81.5B/yr**; IC3 2025 — the FBI's
  **first-ever AI category: 22,364 complaints, $893M** (⚠ ignore the
  viral "1,210% growth" derivative — junk sourcing). Color: the Arup
  deepfake heist — an employee wired $25.6M after a video call where
  **every colleague on screen was fake**. McAfee (⚠ vendor): a voice
  clone needs ~3 seconds of audio.
- **Exhibit 3 — the AI is worse TO vulnerable users (the killer):** MIT,
  AAAI/Feb 2026 — GPT-4, Claude 3 Opus, and Llama 3 give **less accurate
  answers to less-educated and non-native-English users**; Claude
  refused ~11% of questions from less-educated non-native speakers vs
  3.6% for controls, and hit a condescending tone **43.7% vs under 1%**.
  The models infer who you are from how you write and serve you
  accordingly. Pair with sycophancy: Stanford's ELEPHANT — models told

### T — Secure enclaves: the debt gets paid (Tyler + Jackson, ≤8 min — explainer, tied to live stakes)

- **TEE 101 in plain words (Tyler, ≤3 min):** a secure enclave is a
  locked room inside the chip — code runs where even the machine's owner
  and OS can't watch or reach in; the chip signs a receipt
  (attestation) proving what ran. Phone Face-ID data, hardware wallets,
  and now cloud "confidential computing" all live on this idea.
- **The live stake — agent wallets (Jackson):** **MetaMask's Agent
  Wallet** is TEE-backed: keys isolated in an enclave (Cubist /
  Nitro-style), the enclave signs and enforces **spend, scope, and
  frequency policies** — for AI agents including coding agents. This is
  the "wallet-layer spend limits, not prompt-layer" install the show has
  been pointing at since Ep 11's CHAINDROP beat. With EURR putting
  agent-payable balances in 80M consumer wallets, the enclave is what
  stands between an over-trusted agent and the money. (⚠ ~4 weeks old,
  GA'd early Aug — it's the peg we have; no fresh TEE attack landed this
  window.)
- **The anti-example the show already covered:** Coldcard. The enclave
  didn't fail — **the seed-generation math did** ("born-weak seeds,"
  Ep 11). Update while here: Coinkite shipped fixed firmware
  (5.6.1/1.5.1Q) with the honest caveat that **updating does not repair
  an existing seed — move funds**; class actions still threatened, none
  filed (don't say "faces charges"); ⚠ Bitcoin Magazine claims AI was
  likely used to FIND the flaw — single-sourced, say "reportedly."
  Lesson: the enclave is only as good as what you put inside it.
- **The honest ceiling (don't oversell):** physical-access attacks —
  Battering RAM, TEE.fail (2025 research) — broke confidential-computing
  guarantees on major CPUs with ~$50 hardware. Enclaves defend against
  remote software and curious clouds, not a determined attacker holding
  the machine. Threat models matter; say so.
